Taxi or private hire (Mietwagen)?

Taxis and private hire cars (Mietwagen) look similar on the street. Legally, they are two different types of transport with their own rules. The exam almost always has a question on this. Many people mix up the duty to return (Rückkehrpflicht) and the obligation to carry (Beförderungspflicht). If you drive for Uber or Bolt, you almost always drive a Mietwagen.

Exam area 1.1 · Passenger transport lawapprox. 25 minutes§§ 1, 2, 46, 47, 49, 50 PBefG§§ 26, 28, 30 BOKraftDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • recognise when a trip falls under the Passenger Transport Act (PBefG)
  • name the four types of occasional transport
  • tell taxi and Mietwagen apart in 9 points
  • explain the duty to return and its exceptions
  • understand what this means for app trips via Uber, Bolt and similar

When does the Passenger Transport Act apply?

The Passenger Transport Act (Personenbeförderungsgesetz, PBefG) applies when someone carries people in a motor vehicle for payment or on a business basis (entgeltlich oder geschäftsmäßig). Payment does not only mean money. Other economic benefits also count.

Anyone covered by the PBefG needs a licence (Genehmigung) from the authority. Without a licence the trip is forbidden.

§ 2 (1) PBefG (simplified)

Anyone who carries passengers as described in § 1 must have a licence. They are then an “operator” (Unternehmer) under the Act.

When does the PBefG not apply?

For trips by car there are two important exceptions § 1 (2) No. 1 PBefG:

  • The trip is free of charge (unentgeltlich).
  • The total payment is not higher than the amount in the Federal Travel Expenses Act, currently 30 cents per kilometre § 5 (2) BRKG. Example: a ride share where everyone only shares the fuel costs.

Another exception applies to ambulances if patients need medical care during the trip § 1 (2) No. 2 PBefG.

Scheduled and occasional transport

The PBefG distinguishes two big groups. Scheduled transport (Linienverkehr) runs on a fixed route with a timetable, like a city bus. Everything else is occasional transport (Gelegenheitsverkehr). The law allows only four forms of it § 46 (2) PBefG:

Taxis Verkehr mit Taxen§ 47 PBefG
Excursions and holiday tripsby coach§ 48 PBefG
Hired coaches and private hire cars Mietomnibusse und Mietwagen§ 49 PBefG
Pooled on-demand transport gebündelter Bedarfsverkehrshared rides on request, “pooling”§ 50 PBefG

For this exam, the taxi and the Mietwagen matter most. You should know pooled on-demand transport so you can tell it apart.

The duty to return for Mietwagen

This is the most important rule for Mietwagen. It prevents private hire cars from waiting for customers on the street like taxis. After every trip there is only one question:

Step 1The passenger has arrived. The trip is over.
→
QuestionWas there a new booking before or during the trip that came in via the registered office?
→
YesThe driver goes straight to the next passenger.
NoThe driver returns to the registered office without delay (unverzüglich). Waiting at the roadside, at the station or at the airport is forbidden.
§ 49 (4) PBefG (simplified)

Mietwagen may only carry out bookings received at the registered office or the operator's home. After the trip the Mietwagen returns to the registered office without delay, unless it received a new booking from its office before the trip or by phone during the trip.

Exceptions and special rules

  • New booking: If the booking came via the registered office before or during the trip, the driver may continue directly.
  • Large rural municipalities: The authority may allow Mietwagen to wait at other approved parking locations (Abstellorte). The basis is a distance of at least 15 km between the registered office and the parking location, or between several locations. § 49 (5) PBefG
  • Large cities: In cities with more than 100,000 inhabitants the authority can apply extra rules if app-arranged Mietwagen trips make up more than 25 % of occasional transport trips. § 49 (4) PBefG
Remember

Taxi = may look for customers on the street. Mietwagen = only by booking via the registered office, then back.

What does this mean for Uber, Bolt and co.?

In Germany, platforms like Uber, Bolt or FreeNow are usually intermediaries (Vermittler). They do not hold a licence for the trips themselves. The trips are carried out by a Mietwagen company with its own licence, or by a taxi company.

For you as a Mietwagen operator this means:

  • You need your own Mietwagen licence and therefore this exam.
  • Every app booking must come in via your registered office and be recorded there. Electronic records are allowed.
  • The duty to return also applies to app trips.
  • The platform keeps a commission (Provision). You must include it in your pricing. You learn how in lesson 2.2.
Warning

Carrying passengers for money without a licence is an administrative offence and can be very expensive. More in lesson 1.2.

Typical mistakes

!“A Mietwagen may wait for the next customer at the airport.”Without a new booking via the registered office, the duty to return applies.
!“Mietwagen also have an obligation to carry.”The obligation to carry only applies to taxis, and only in the mandatory service area.
!“A taxi may wait for customers anywhere.”Only at approved ranks in the municipality of the registered office, unless the authority allows more.
!“A Mietwagen needs a taximeter.”It needs an odometer (Wegstreckenzähler).
!“In a taxi I can negotiate the price freely.”The taxi tariff is binding. Deviations only where the regulation allows them.
