Trips abroad

From Aachen it is only a few minutes to the Netherlands and Belgium. Cross-border trips are everyday business for Aachen operators: to Maastricht airport, to Vaals, Kerkrade, Eupen or Liège. But what may a German taxi do abroad? Taking passengers there: yes. Driving around there: no. This lesson explains the basic rules. Exam area 5 is only tested where it matters for the IHK district, so in Aachen very likely.

Exam area 5.1 · Cross-border transportapprox. 25 minutes§§ 52, 53 PBefG§ 12 UStGLaw of the neighbouring countriesDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • explain which rules apply to cross-border trips by taxi and Mietwagen
  • explain the term cabotage (Kabotage)
  • judge which trips to the Netherlands and Belgium are allowed
  • correctly classify tariff, price and taxes on trips abroad

The principle

For cross-border trips by motor vehicle, the PBefG applies on the German part of the route, whether the operator comes from Germany or abroad § 52 (1) PBefG. On the foreign part of the route the law of the other country applies.

  • For taxis and Mietwagen there is no uniform EU law as there is for buses. Each country decides for itself what foreign taxis may do there.
  • Foreign operators do not need a German licence for occasional transport to Germany if an agreement with their country covers this. Otherwise the Federal Ministry of Transport can allow an exception § 52 (3) PBefG.
  • The border police and customs can turn back vehicles that do not carry a required licence § 52 (4) PBefG.
  • Pure transit through Germany is governed by § 53 PBefG.

What is cabotage?

Cabotage (Kabotage) means: a foreign vehicle carries passengers within another country, i.e. from one place in the Netherlands to another place in the Netherlands. For taxis and Mietwagen, cabotage is forbidden in the neighbouring countries.

Trip with an Aachen taxiAllowed?
Aachen → Maastricht (passenger gets in in Aachen)yes
Maastricht → Aachen, pre-bookedyes under Dutch law only with a prior booking
Maastricht → Aachen, passenger hails the taxi spontaneously in Maastrichtno
Vaals → Maastricht (both places in the Netherlands)no cabotage
Aachen → Eupen (Belgium), then back emptyyes
Eupen → Liège (both in Belgium)no cabotage
Aachen → through the Netherlands → Aachen (shortcut)yes transit

The other way round, Dutch taxis may bring passengers to Germany, but may not carry them within Germany. In the Netherlands cabotage is punished with fines of about 2.000 €; if the driver doesn't pay, the vehicle can be held.

Check the current rules

The rules of the neighbouring countries can change, and in Belgium the regions (Wallonia, Flanders, Brussels) are responsible. Before regular trips abroad, ask the licensing authority, the taxi association or the Euregio cross-border information service.

Taxi and Mietwagen across the border

  • Taxi tariff: the German tariff only applies within the mandatory service area (Pflichtfahrbereich). For trips abroad you agree the price before the trip starts, unless the local taxi regulations say otherwise.
  • Duty to carry: only applies within the mandatory service area. A taxi may in principle refuse a trip abroad.
  • Taxi sign: outside the tariff area it is not lit as “free”. You may not wait for customers abroad.
  • Mietwagen: on trips abroad too, the booking must come via the registered office, and afterwards the car returns to the registered office. A Mietwagen may not wait for new customers abroad.

VAT

On cross-border trips only the part of the route in Germany is taxed in Germany. The reduced rate for taxis applies under the usual rules (municipality or up to 50 km). The part abroad falls under the tax law of the other country. If you drive abroad regularly, ask your tax adviser whether you need to register there.

Typical mistakes

!“I can join the taxis in Vaals, Vaals is right next to Aachen.”Standby only in your own municipality in Germany. Abroad: cabotage.
!“My Aachen taxi tariff applies abroad.”Only in the mandatory service area. For trips abroad: agree the price beforehand.
!“The EU bus regulation applies to taxis.”There is no EU regulation for taxis; national law applies.
