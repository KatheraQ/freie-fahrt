Working time, breaks and rest periods

Driving a taxi often means long shifts, nights and weekends. For your employed drivers the Working Hours Act (Arbeitszeitgesetz) sets firm limits on working time, breaks and rest periods. Many confuse this with the EU driving time rules for lorries and buses. Those do not apply to taxis and Mietwagen.

Exam area 1.4 · Employment lawapprox. 30 minutes§§ 2–6, 9–11, 16 ArbZGReg. (EC) 561/2006§ 17 MiLoGDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • name the maximum working time and how it is balanced
  • apply the break rules
  • explain the rest period and the exception for transport companies
  • know the rules for Sunday and night work
  • explain why EU driving and rest times and the tachograph do not apply to taxis

Who does the Working Hours Act apply to?

The Working Hours Act (ArbZG) protects employees. It applies to your employed drivers and office staff, including mini-jobbers. It does not apply to you as a self-employed operator § 2 (2) ArbZG. Still: fatigue is one of the most common causes of accidents. Don't drive when you are tired.

Working time is the time from the start to the end of work, without breaks § 2 (1) ArbZG. For taxis this includes waiting time at the rank, vehicle cleaning and cashing up.

Maximum working time

8 hoursper working day (Monday to Saturday) as a rule.
up to 10 hoursare allowed if an average of 8 hours per working day is not exceeded over 6 calendar months or 24 weeks.
48 hoursper week on average (6 working days × 8 hours).

§ 3 ArbZG

You must record working time over 8 hours per working day and keep the records for at least 2 years § 16 (2) ArbZG. In the taxi trade you must record all working time anyway because of § 17 MiLoG (lesson 1.6).

Breaks

Working time per dayMinimum break
up to 6 hoursnot required
more than 6 up to 9 hours30 minutes
more than 9 hours45 minutes
  • Breaks can be split into parts of at least 15 minutes.
  • Nobody may work more than 6 hours in a row without a break.
  • The break is fixed in advance. Waiting time at the taxi rank is not a break if the driver must be ready to leave at any time.

§ 4 ArbZG

Rest period between two shifts

After the end of work the employee needs an uninterrupted rest period of at least 11 hours § 5 (1) ArbZG.

Exception for transport companies

In transport companies (Verkehrsbetriebe), which includes taxi and Mietwagen businesses, the rest period may be shortened by up to 1 hour to 10 hours. Each shortening must be balanced within one calendar month or 4 weeks by extending another rest period to at least 12 hours § 5 (2) ArbZG.

Sundays, public holidays and night work

In principle employees may not work on Sundays and public holidays § 9 ArbZG. Transport companies are an exception if the work cannot be done on working days § 10 (1) No. 10 ArbZG. So taxis may also run on Sundays. In return § 11 ArbZG:

  • At least 15 Sundays per year must stay free.
  • Anyone who works on a Sunday gets a substitute day off within 2 weeks.
  • Anyone who works on a public holiday falling on a weekday gets a substitute day off within 8 weeks.

Night work

Night time is from 11 pm to 6 am. Night work is work that covers more than 2 hours of night time. For night workers §§ 2, 6 ArbZG:

  • Working time in principle at most 8 hours; up to 10 hours only if balanced within 1 calendar month or 4 weeks.
  • Right to an occupational health check (regularly every 3 years, from age 50 every year).
  • Without a collective agreement: a reasonable number of paid days off or a reasonable pay supplement.

Driving and rest times: do they apply to taxis?

For lorries and buses there are EU rules on driving and rest times with a tachograph Reg. (EC) 561/2006. In passenger transport they only apply to vehicles built for more than 9 persons including the driver.

Remember for the exam

Taxis and Mietwagen are cars with at most 9 seats including the driver. EU driving and rest times do not apply to them, and they need no tachograph. For employed drivers the Working Hours Act applies instead.

If your business also runs minibuses with more than 9 seats (hired coaches), the EU rules, the Drivers Act (FPersG) and a separate licence apply to them.

Typical mistakes

!“Taxi drivers must keep EU driving times and need a tachograph.”Only for vehicles for more than 9 people. For taxis the ArbZG applies.
!“With 8 hours of work, 15 minutes of break are enough.”More than 6 hours: 30 minutes; more than 9 hours: 45 minutes.
!“8 hours between two shifts are enough.”11 hours; in transport companies 10 hours with compensation to 12 hours.
!“Working on Sunday gives no right to compensation.”Substitute day off within 2 weeks, 15 free Sundays per year.
