Bookkeeping, cash book and profit calculation

The tax office looks particularly closely at the taxi trade because a lot is paid in cash. You must record your income completely, keep a cash book and calculate your profit correctly. In this lesson you learn who must keep which books, how long to keep what, how a cash book works and how cash-basis accounting (Einnahmen-Überschuss-Rechnung) is structured.

Exam area 2.5 · Bookkeepingapprox. 40 minutes§ 238 HGB, § 241a HGB§§ 140, 141, 146, 146a, 147 AO§ 4 (3), §§ 6, 7, 11 EStG§ 22 UStGGoBDDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • explain who must do double-entry bookkeeping and who may use cash-basis accounting
  • name the retention periods for books, vouchers and letters
  • keep a cash book correctly and avoid typical mistakes
  • draw up a simple cash-basis profit calculation
  • apply depreciation, low-value assets and the cash principle
  • know the basics of double-entry bookkeeping (inventory, balance sheet, accounts, booking entry)

For all operators: record turnover for VAT § 22 UStG, and record cash receipts individually § 146 (1) AO. The principles for proper digital bookkeeping are in the GoBD: complete, correct, timely, orderly and unalterable.

Retention periods

DocumentsPeriod
books, records, inventories, annual accounts, opening balance sheet10 years
accounting vouchers: invoices, receipts, bank statements, taximeter printouts8 years
business letters received and sent, other tax-relevant documents6 years

§ 147 (1) and (3) AO

The period starts at the end of the calendar year in which the document was created § 147 (4) AO. A receipt from 3 February 2026 may therefore be destroyed on 1 January 2035 at the earliest (end of 2026 + 8 years). Digital data must remain readable and available for an audit during the whole period.

Don't confuse

Bookings in Mietwagen transport: 1 year (§ 49 (4) PBefG). Working time records: 2 years (§ 17 MiLoG). Tax vouchers: 8 years, books 10 years.

The cash book

Anyone who takes a lot of cash needs a cash book (Kassenbuch). It contains all cash receipts and payments with date and voucher, and after each day the cash balance.

The most important rules

  • Enter daily and promptly, in chronological order.
  • Individual records: every trip separately. In a taxi the taximeter with TSE does this (lesson 1.10).
  • No negative cash balance. You cannot take more out of a till than is in it. A negative balance tells an auditor at once: income is missing here.
  • Ready for a cash count (kassensturzfähig): the counted cash must match the book at any time.
  • No later changes that can no longer be seen. No correction fluid, no spreadsheet that can be freely overwritten.
  • Also enter private withdrawals and private deposits.

Shift sheets

For every shift the driver records: date, driver, vehicle, mileage at start and end, occupied km, number of trips and receipts (cash, card, invoice). These shift sheets (Schichtzettel) and the taximeter data are primary records and must be kept.

Cash-basis accounting (EÜR)

Profit = business income − business expenses
  • Cash principle: income counts when the money arrives. Expenses count when they are paid § 11 EStG. An invoice to a health insurer from December that is paid in January is income in January.
  • Exception: regularly recurring payments (e.g. VAT advance payments, rent) made up to 10 days before or after the turn of the year count for the year they belong to.
  • The EÜR is submitted electronically to the tax office with the form Anlage EÜR.
  • VAT: VAT collected is business income; input VAT paid and VAT paid to the tax office are business expenses.

Purchases: depreciation instead of an immediate expense

A new taxi is not a business expense in one year. The costs are spread over the useful life: depreciation (for tax: AfA) § 7 EStG.

  • Useful life according to the official depreciation table. For cars in taxi and Mietwagen transport the sector table for passenger transport applies: 5 years. For tax purposes there is no residual value.
  • In the year of purchase only pro rata for the full months from the month of purchase.
  • Low-value assets up to 800 € net (e.g. a card terminal) may be deducted in full immediately § 6 (2) EStG.

The imputed owner's salary from cost accounting does not appear in the EÜR. Your “wage” is the profit.

Basics of double-entry bookkeeping

Stocktaking InventurCounting, measuring, weighing all assets and debts on a key date (usually 31.12.).
Inventory InventarThe result of stocktaking: detailed list of assets and debts.
Balance sheet BilanzShort comparison: on the left assets (vehicles, cash, bank, receivables), on the right liabilities and equity (equity, debts). Both sides are equal.
P&L GuVProfit and loss account: income minus expenses of the year.
Journal GrundbuchAll entries in chronological order.
General ledger HauptbuchThe same entries sorted by account.
Account KontoT-shaped record with debit (Soll, left) and credit (Haben, right).
Booking entry Buchungssatz“Debit to credit” (Soll an Haben). Every entry touches at least two accounts.
Examples of booking entries
Fuel paid in cash, 50 € net + 9,50 € VAT: Fuel costs 50,00 / Input VAT 9,50 to Cash 59,50 Cash receipts of a shift 107 € gross (7 %): Cash 107,00 to Revenue 100,00 / VAT 7,00 Cash taken to the bank, 500 €: Bank 500,00 to Cash 500,00

Typical mistakes

!Cash book filled in from memory at the end of the month.Daily and promptly, with a voucher for every entry.
!Cash balance goes negative.Impossible in a real till. The tax office then estimates your income.
!“I must keep vouchers for 10 years.”Accounting vouchers since 2025: 8 years. 10 years apply to books and annual accounts.
!New taxi deducted as an expense in the year of purchase.Depreciation over the useful life (taxi: 5 years).
!Owner's salary entered as an expense in the EÜR.Only in cost accounting. The EÜR has no wage for the owner.
