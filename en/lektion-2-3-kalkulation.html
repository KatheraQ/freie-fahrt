Calculating fares and quotes

In lesson 2.2 you calculated your costs. Now you turn them into a price: for a quote to a company, an airport flat rate or a Mietwagen trip via an app. You learn the pricing scheme, the price floors and how to check whether a price set by an app covers your costs at all.

Exam area 2.3 · Fares and conditionsapprox. 35 minutesPBZugV Annex 3 No. 2.2DIHK syllabus 2.3§§ 51, 51a PBefGDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • apply the pricing scheme from cost price to gross price
  • explain which costs the base fare, km price and time price cover
  • distinguish short-term and long-term price floor
  • calculate a quote for a Mietwagen trip
  • check with reverse calculation whether an app's fixed price covers the costs

The pricing scheme

With forward calculation (Vorwärtskalkulation) you start from your costs and work out the price the customer pays:

Cost price of the trip (from cost accounting)100,00 €
+ profit mark-up (profit and risk), e.g. 12 %12,00 €
= net price112,00 €
+ VAT (Mietwagen 19 %)21,28 €
= gross price (quote to private customers)133,28 €

To business customers you usually quote the net price plus VAT. Private customers must always be given the final price including VAT.

Where does the cost price come from?

From cost accounting (lesson 2.2). Typically two rates:

  • cost per km for the distance, including the empty return trip if there is one.
  • cost per hour for waiting time, mainly driver's wage and owner's salary.

What does each part of the taxi tariff cover?

The taxi tariff is also a calculation. The authority sets it, but often on application by the trade with a cost calculation. Each component covers certain costs:

Componentmainly covers
Base fare Grundpreiscosts that arise for every trip regardless of length: driving to the customer, waiting at the rank, getting in and out, billing
Price per km Kilometerpreisdistance-related costs: fuel, tyres, maintenance, depreciation, and the empty kilometres that must be paid for too
Time price / waiting Zeitpreistime-related costs: driver's wage and owner's salary when the taxi stands still or is in a traffic jam
Surcharges Zuschlägespecial costs, e.g. a large vehicle, a lot of luggage

If your costs rise, for example because of a higher minimum wage or more expensive fuel, the trade can apply to the authority for a tariff adjustment.

Price floors

Long-term price floor= cost price (all fixed and variable costs). Below it you make a loss in the long run.
Short-term price floor= variable costs. Below it you lose additional money with every trip. Above it every trip contributes to the fixed costs (positive contribution margin).
Example (sample values)

A hotel asks for a fixed price for trips to the station (6 km, with empty return 12 km). Your cost price: 0,95 € per km, of which 0,20 € per km variable.

Cost price = 12 km × 0,95 € = 11,40 € → long-term floor (net) Variable costs = 12 km × 0,20 € = 2,40 € → short-term floor (net) The hotel offers 9,00 € net: → covers the variable costs (contribution margin 6,60 € per trip), but not the cost price. Only sensible to fill idle times.
Careful with taxis

Within the mandatory service area the tariff applies. You may only agree a fixed price with the hotel as a special agreement under § 51 (2) PBefG, or within a fixed price / price range for pre-booked trips (lesson 1.3).

A quote for a Mietwagen trip

A company wants you to pick up a guest at Düsseldorf airport and bring him to Aachen. You calculate like this:

Working (sample values)
Empty drive Aachen → Düsseldorf 85 km Trip with guest Düsseldorf → Aachen 85 km Total km 170 km Cost per km (incl. driver) 1,10 € Waiting at the airport (delay) 1 h × 32,00 € Distance costs = 170 km × 1,10 € = 187,00 € Waiting time = 1 h × 32,00 € = 32,00 € Cost price = 219,00 € + profit mark-up 15 % = 32,85 € Net price = 251,85 € + 19 % VAT = 47,85 € Gross price = 299,70 €

Common mistakes: forgetting the empty trip, calculating with 7 % instead of 19 % (Mietwagen!), or adding the mark-up to the gross price. Practise more quotes in the calculation trainer.

Reverse calculation: is the app price worth it?

For many app trips the platform sets the price. Then you calculate backwards (Rückwärtskalkulation): what is left for you after VAT and commission?

Example (sample values)

The passenger pays 23,80 € in the app for 9 km. Plus 3 km to drive to the customer. In this example the platform keeps 25 % of the net price. Your costs: 0,90 € per km driven.

Gross price 23,80 € − 19 % VAT (23,80 × 19 ÷ 119) − 3,80 € = Net price 20,00 € − 25 % commission − 5,00 € = Revenue for you 15,00 € Costs: (9 km + 3 km approach) × 0,90 € = 10,80 € Result: 15,00 € − 10,80 € = + 4,20 € per trip ✓

Always count all km: the drive to the customer and, if no follow-up booking comes, the return to the office (duty to return!).

Check your contract

Platforms calculate the commission differently, sometimes on the net, sometimes on the gross price, sometimes with fixed fees. In the exam, the task tells you how to calculate.

Typical mistakes

!Profit mark-up added to the gross price.Order: cost price → + profit → net → + VAT → gross.
!Empty drive to the pick-up forgotten.All km driven count, also without a passenger.
!Mietwagen priced with 7 % VAT.Mietwagen: 19 %.
!“I never drive below cost price.”Short term a trip above variable costs can make sense. Long term all costs must be covered.

Summary

Forwardcost price + mark-up = net; + VAT = gross
Reversegross − VAT = net; − commission = revenue; compare with costs
Tariffbase fare: costs per trip; km price: distance incl. empty km; time price: wages when standing
Price floorslong-term: cost price; short-term: variable costs
Taxideviation from tariff only as a special agreement or fixed price/range per tariff
