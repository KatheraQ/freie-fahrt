Payments, financing and key figures

How do your passengers pay, and what does it cost you? How do you finance a new vehicle: loan or leasing? And how can you tell whether your business is healthy? This lesson covers payments, loans and collateral, and the most important key figures. The exam often has small calculations on interest, cash discounts (Skonto) and profitability.

Exam area 2.1 · Paymentsapprox. 40 minutes§§ 286, 288, 488, 675x, 676b BGBReg. (EU) 2024/886 (instant payments)DIHK syllabus 2.1Deutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • know the payment methods in a taxi and their pros and cons
  • explain bank transfer, instant transfer and SEPA direct debit
  • distinguish types of loans, leasing and collateral
  • calculate interest and the benefit of a cash discount
  • calculate and interpret equity ratio, profitability and liquidity

Whether a taxi must accept card payment can be set in the local tariff. The regulation may set the method of payment § 51 (1) sentence 2 No. 5 PBefG. In many cities card payment is now mandatory.

Transfers and direct debits

SEPA transfer ÜberweisungYou instruct your bank to send money, using the payee's IBAN. Credited at the latest on the next bank working day.
Instant transfer EchtzeitüberweisungMoney reaches the payee within 10 seconds, around the clock. Since 9 October 2025 all banks in the euro area must offer it, and it may not cost more than a normal transfer.Reg. (EU) 2024/886
Verification of payeeSince October 2025 the bank checks before every transfer whether name and IBAN match. If not, it warns you.
SEPA direct debit LastschriftThe payee collects the money. For this he needs a written mandate from the payer. Good for regular payments, e.g. radio fee, insurance, vehicle tax.

Reversing a direct debit

  • SEPA core direct debit: the payer can reclaim an authorised debit within 8 weeks without giving reasons § 675x BGB.
  • If there was no mandate at all (unauthorised), even up to 13 months § 676b BGB.
  • SEPA business-to-business direct debit: only between businesses, no refund after debiting.

When a customer doesn't pay

A debtor is in default (Verzug) if he does not pay after a reminder, but at the latest 30 days after the due date and receipt of the invoice. For consumers this only applies if the invoice says so § 286 (3) BGB. You can then claim default interest: 5 percentage points above the base rate for consumers, 9 percentage points for businesses, plus a flat fee of 40 € for businesses § 288 BGB. If the customer still doesn't pay, the court dunning procedure (Mahnbescheid) helps.

Leasing

With leasing you use a vehicle against monthly payments. The leasing company remains the owner. At the end you return it or buy it.

  • Advantages: little own capital needed, payments are deductible business expenses, predictable costs, often with service.
  • Disadvantages: usually more expensive overall than buying, mileage limit (important for taxis with high mileage!), extra payments for extra km or damage.
  • Mileage leasing: fixed price for an agreed mileage. Residual value leasing: you bear the risk that the car is worth less at the end than planned.

Calculating interest and cash discounts

In commercial calculations the year has 360 days and each month 30 days.

Interest = capital × rate × days ÷ (100 × 360)
Example
Overdraft 8.000 €, 11 % interest, 45 days Interest = 8.000 × 11 × 45 ÷ (100 × 360) = 110,00 €

Is a cash discount worth it?

An invoice says: “Payable within 30 days net, 2 % cash discount (Skonto) if paid within 10 days.” If you pay on day 10 you save 2 %, but give up 20 days of payment term. As an annual interest rate:

Cash discount annual rate ≈ discount rate × 360 ÷ (payment term − discount period)
2 % × 360 ÷ (30 − 10) = 2 × 18 = 36 % per year
Remember

A cash discount is almost always a very good deal. It is even worth briefly using the overdraft (e.g. 11 %) for it.

Case study (sample values)

A taxi business with 3 vehicles has at the end of the year:

Equity40.000 €
Debt (of which short-term 15.000 €)60.000 €
Cash and bank9.000 €
Receivables (e.g. health insurers)12.000 €
Turnover (net)240.000 €
Profit30.000 €
Interest on debt3.000 €
Total capital = 40.000 + 60.000 = 100.000 € Equity ratio = 40.000 ÷ 100.000 × 100 = 40 % Return on equity = 30.000 ÷ 40.000 × 100 = 75 % Return on total capital = (30.000 + 3.000) ÷ 100.000 = 33 % Return on sales = 30.000 ÷ 240.000 × 100 = 12,5 % Liquidity 1st degree = 9.000 ÷ 15.000 × 100 = 60 % Liquidity 2nd degree = (9.000 + 12.000) ÷ 15.000 = 140 %

In small sole proprietorships the return on equity often looks very high because the profit still contains the owner's own wage. For a fair comparison, deduct the imputed owner's salary.

Financial plan

A liquidity plan lists all expected income and expenses for the coming months. This shows you in time when money will be tight, for example in the month when insurance, vehicle tax and the roadworthiness test fall due. Banks often ask for it before granting a loan.

Typical mistakes

!Interest calculated with 365 days although commercial calculation was asked.Commercial: 360 days, month = 30 days.
!“2 % cash discount is only 2 % saving, hardly worth it.”Per year that is about 36 %.
!“With transfer of ownership as security the bank takes the car.”The bank becomes owner, you remain in possession and keep driving. The bank keeps the vehicle title.
!Liquidity 2nd degree calculated without receivables.1st degree: only cash. 2nd degree: plus short-term receivables.
