Passports and customs

In the Schengen area there are normally no border checks. But since September 2024 Germany has been checking again at all land borders, including near Aachen. Drivers and passengers need valid ID. And anyone who takes goods across the border must know what is allowed. Especially important for taxi drivers in Aachen: cannabis from the Netherlands.

Exam area 5.2 · Passports and customsapprox. 20 minutesSchengen Borders CodeCustoms law, ZollVGBtMG, KCanGWaffGDeutsche Version

After this lesson you can

  • name the ID documents needed for drivers and passengers
  • know what border checks in the Schengen area mean
  • distinguish the rules for goods within the EU and from non-EU countries
  • name forbidden and restricted goods

ID documents

PersonWhich document?
Germans and other EU citizensvalid ID card or passport
Non-EU citizens living in Germanypassport and residence permit
Non-EU citizens as visitorspassport, depending on the country with a visa (Schengen visa: usually up to 90 days in 180 days)
Childrentheir own ID card or passport (child passport)

A driving licence is not an ID document. The driver needs his ID card or passport on every trip anyway (§ 2a SchwarzArbG).

Border checks

Germany, the Netherlands and Belgium are part of the Schengen area. Normally there are no checks at the borders. But since 16 September 2024 Germany has been checking temporarily at all land borders. The checks have been extended several times. So expect the Federal Police to check IDs at the border.

Passenger without papers

If a passenger has no valid ID or visa, there can be problems at a check. Anyone who knowingly takes people without a right of residence across the border and takes money for it can commit the offence of smuggling people (§ 96 AufenthG). If in doubt, don't drive.

Goods across the border

Within the EU (e.g. NL, BE)

In the EU single market you may in principle take goods for your own use freely. For tobacco and alcohol there are guide quantities (Richtmengen). If you carry more, you must be able to show that it is for your own use:

Cigarettes800 pieces
Cigarillos / cigars400 / 200 pieces
Smoking tobacco1 kg
Spirits10 litres
Intermediate products (e.g. sherry)20 litres
Wine (of which sparkling wine max. 60 l)90 litres
Beer110 litres

Cash of 10.000 € or more must be declared on request by customs when travelling within the EU. When entering or leaving the EU it must be declared in writing.

From non-EU countries (e.g. Switzerland)

Stricter duty-free allowances apply here, for example 200 cigarettes, 1 litre of spirits and other goods up to a value of 300 € (arrival by land). Above that, duties are due. This is rare for Aachen operators, but it can come up in the exam.

Forbidden and restricted goods

GoodsRule
Narcotics (drugs)Import forbidden and a criminal offence (BtMG).
CannabisIn Germany adults may possess small amounts since 2024. But importing it, even from a Dutch coffee shop, is forbidden (KCanG).
Weapons and ammunitionOnly with a permit; within the EU with the European Firearms Pass (WaffG).
Explosives, fireworksForbidden or strongly restricted; foreign fireworks without German approval are forbidden.
Counterfeit brand goods, protected animals and plantsForbidden or only with a permit.
Medicinesonly for personal use (usually up to 3 months).
As a taxi driver

Dangerous substances (explosive, highly flammable) are excluded from transport anyway (§ 15 BOKraft). If you notice that a passenger wants to take forbidden goods across the border, you may refuse the trip. Never knowingly help with smuggling.
